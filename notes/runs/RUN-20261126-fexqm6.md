## Notes

`spikes/key-hierarchy` builds ADR-0005's hierarchy with real primitives: age X25519 wraps
of UK to devices, of DEKs to the server's delegate key and for the append key;
XChaCha20-Poly1305 for DEKs under UK and for items, with associated data binding account,
class, item id and key version; Argon2id (64 MiB, 3 passes) for the recovery wrap; an
ADR-0007 log, hash-chained with SHA-256 and each entry signed with Ed25519, checked by
devices against checkpoints. The scenarios are tests (`tests.txt`), the timings a
release binary over 100k items of 2 KiB (`timings.txt`), on rogue (Apple silicon).

What it showed:
- Prediction 1 holds: with a calendar-only delegate the server writes and reads calendar
  items while every device is offline; mail it receives is sealed to the account's append
  key and unreadable to it, as is older mail under the mail DEK; a device coming online
  reads the sealed mail and moves it under the DEK; withdrawing consent ends access.
- Prediction 2 holds: each delegate unwrap, encryption and decryption the server makes,
  allowed or denied, is one entry. An edited entry, a deleted one and a truncated log fail
  verification; a history rewritten and re-signed with the server's own key verifies on
  its own and is caught only by a device's checkpoint, so checkpoints are what make the
  log trustworthy against the admin.
- Prediction 3 fails as ADR-0005 is written. Rotating UK and re-wrapping the DEKs leaves
  the DEKs unchanged, so a revoked device that kept the account's wraps (it could read
  them while enrolled) still opens data written after its revocation (test
  `revocation_by_rewrap_only_…`). Rotating each DEK to a new version for new writes, and
  the append key with it, shuts the device out of everything new and loses nothing; old
  items stay under their old versions, which the device could read anyway (test
  `revocation_with_dek_rotation_…`). Either way revocation takes ~70 ms for one account,
  nearly all of it the Argon2id re-wrap of the recovery key, and touches no item.
- Prediction 4 holds by far: ~6 µs to encrypt and ~5 µs to decrypt a 2 KiB item.
- Recovery after every device is lost works from the passphrase alone; a wrong one fails.

What the server's admin can read, by state:
- No delegate for a class, every device offline: nothing of that class; new items arrive
  sealed to the append key. Metadata (sizes, times, counts, which class) is visible.
- A delegate consented for a class, the service running: everything of that class,
  through the delegate key in the service's memory; each use is in the log, and a log
  rewritten by the admin is caught by a device's checkpoint, not prevented.
- A device unlocked: nothing more on the server; UK lives on the device.
- After revocation with DEK rotation: the revoked device reads only what existed before.

Not controlled: timings are rogue's, not ghost001's (an Ampere A1, slower per core; at
~6 µs a slowdown of 100x would still meet the prediction); the spike holds keys in
memory with `Zeroizing`, with no TPM or systemd credentials; device certificates and the
log's checkpoint protocol are out of its scope.
